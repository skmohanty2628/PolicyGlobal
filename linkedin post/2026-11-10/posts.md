## Post 1 (post1.png)
An insurer can report a better quarter and still be about to disappear.

Safety Insurance Group posted a 96.3% combined ratio for Q3, down from 98.9%. Net income was $30.0 million. Shareholders have already approved a $1.54 billion cash sale to Mapfre at $105 a share. The last step is Massachusetts regulatory approval.

What stood out:
✔ Loss ratio fell to 67.2% from 70.4%
✔ Premiums dipped 4.2%, but investment income rose 15%
✔ Nine-month combined ratio is still 101.7% after a rough winter

When a company is being acquired, do you read its results differently?

Trisura Group's Q3 net income was $26.2 million, against $38.6 million a year ago. But operating net income held steady at $35.0 million, and primary lines grew 12.5%.

The numbers behind it:
✔ Combined ratio 87.5%, against 86.0%
✔ Net insurance revenue up 2.9% to $203.1 million
✔ Canadian fronting declined while primary lines grew

Which number do you trust more when judging an insurer, net income or operating income?

## Post 3 (post3.png)
Premiums up 35%. Profit up 124%. That is a rare pairing in health insurance.

Niva Bupa's September quarter profit after tax came in at about ₹136 crore, against ₹60.52 crore a year earlier. Gross written premium reached ₹2,484 crore.

Key facts:
✔ Retail health premium grew 47% to ₹1,876 crore
✔ Combined operating ratio improved to 93.20% from 96.62%
✔ First-half claim settlement ratio: 95.6%

Is fast growth in Indian health insurance sustainable, or does it get harder from here?

## Post 4 (post4.png)
Ask most people what life insurers sell and they say savings. The growth is somewhere else.

ICICI Prudential Life's first-half profit rose 26.5% to ₹760 crore. The interesting line is protection: annualised premium equivalent up 39.5% to ₹1,155 crore, while annuity sales stayed flat.

At a glance:
✔ Value of new business up 22.3% to ₹1,283 crore
✔ VNB margin 26%, up from 24.7%
✔ Solvency ratio 229.9%

Are Indian buyers finally treating term cover as a necessity instead of a tax product?

## Post 5 (post5.png)
Revenue fell 60%. The guidance did not move. Here is why.

eHealth's Q3 revenue was $21.3 million, against $53.9 million a year ago. Medicare Advantage approved members dropped to 11,562 from 28,645. Yet the company reiterated its full-year revenue guidance of $405 million to $445 million.

What that implies:
✔ Marketing spend fell 55% to $10.9 million
✔ Operating cash flow improved to negative $11.2 million
✔ The guidance depends on a very large fourth quarter, when Medicare open enrollment runs

For businesses that earn most of their year in a few weeks, how much should investors trust the guide?

✔ Nine-month net income up 34.8% to $56.0 million

Is the shift from agents to online comparison permanent, or does it swing back when the market softens?
