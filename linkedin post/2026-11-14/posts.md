## Post 1 (post1.png): Europe cat budgets

Who actually paid for 2026's storms? Not the reinsurers.

We put six European insurers' catastrophe budgets side by side. By September:

✅ Swiss Re had used about 40% of its full-year nat cat budget
✅ Hannover Re had used about 74% of its nine-month budget, with roughly EUR 470 million unused
✅ AXA said hail and storm losses broke through its budget

Our reading: this year's losses were thousands of medium-sized hail and storm claims, which stay with the insurers that sell home and car cover. Big reinsurance programmes barely got touched.

Do you expect cheaper reinsurance at January renewals to reach household premiums, or will hail losses cancel it out?

## Post 2 (post2.png): UK fix vs tracker

A 5.97% fix or a 4.68% tracker? We ran the numbers.

On a 200,000 pound, 25-year repayment mortgage:

• Two-year fix at 5.97%: about 1,285 pounds a month
• Two-year tracker at 4.68%: about 1,132 pounds a month
• For the tracker to cost the same over two years, Bank Rate would need to jump about 1.29 points almost at once and stay there

Even with two more quarter-point rises, the tracker still comes out roughly 2,800 pounds ahead before fees. The fix buys certainty, and for some budgets that is worth paying for.

Which would you pick right now, and why?

## Post 3 (post3.png): Medicare 2027

Medicare open enrollment ends December 7. Some of the 2027 numbers are final. Some are still guesses.

Final:
✅ Part D deductible: $700 (from $615)
✅ Part D out-of-pocket cap: $2,400 (from $2,100)

Still projected:
• Part B premium: about $209.50 a month (Trustees estimate)

Our worked example: someone who hits the drug cap pays about $416 more in 2027, and most of it comes from the higher cap.

If you help a parent pick a plan, compare total yearly cost with their actual drug list, not just the monthly premium. What do people get wrong most often at open enrollment?
