## Post 1 (post1.png)
One insurer reported a $420 million loss last quarter. Its own "operating" profit was $349 million. Both numbers are true.

We lined up nine insurers' Q3 results and divided headline net income by each company's operating figure:

✔ Lincoln Financial: about 413%
✔ Prudential and Cincinnati Financial: within about 5 points of 100%
✔ Manulife and Sun Life: about 67% and 69%
✔ Unum and Mercury General: a net loss next to an operating profit

Most of the gap came from investment gains and losses, not from the insurance business itself.

## Post 2 (post2.png)
India's insurers split into two camps this quarter.

Health specialists earned money from underwriting. General insurers mostly did not, and investment income carried their profits. Our Q2 FY27 scorecard:

✔ Niva Bupa 93.2% and Star Health 98.0% combined ratios

✔ Floods added 2.4 points at Go Digit and 1.7 at ICICI Lombard

## Post 4 (post4.png)
Which loan is cheaper: 8% or 7.5%?

In our worked example, the 7.5% loan costs more. Same 20,000, same five years:

✔ Loan A: 8% with a 600 fee. Total cost about 4,932. APR about 9.3%
✔ Loan B: 7.5% with a 1,000 fee. Total cost about 5,046. APR about 9.7%
✔ Repay after two years and the gap doubles

The interest rate sets your payment. The APR shows what the loan costs once fees are counted.
