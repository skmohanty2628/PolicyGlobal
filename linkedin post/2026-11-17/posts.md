## Post 1 (image: post1.png)

A 2% deductible sounds small. On a $350,000 home it is $7,000.

Percentage deductibles for wind, hail and hurricane damage are now common in storm states. Many homeowners only find out what theirs means after the roof is damaged.

✔ 1% of $350,000 is $3,500, 2% is $7,000, 5% is $17,500
✔ On a $15,000 hail claim, a 2% deductible means the insurer pays $8,000
✔ Dropping from 2% to 1% for $300 a year takes about 11.7 years to pay off, if you never claim

We wrote a plain-English guide with worked examples and the questions to ask your insurer.

## Post 2 (image: post2.png)

Microsoft 365 was down for about 18 hours last week. Most of that loss will never reach an insurer.

Loss modellers' estimates of the insured cost run from under $100 million to about $1.4 billion. The bigger story is in the policy wording.

✔ Typical cyber waiting periods are 8 to 12 hours, and losses inside that window are not paid
✔ On our example, a business losing $1,000 an hour keeps $8,000 to $12,000 of an $18,000 loss
✔ Many policies exclude non-malicious failures at a provider altogether

Have you checked whether your cyber policy covers a provider outage that is not an attack?

## Post 3 (image: post3.png)

Commercial insurance in Asia is getting cheaper. Unless you are in Japan, or buying motor cover.

Aon's Q3 data shows a 36-point gap between the market where prices fell most and where they rose.

✔ India property: down more than 31%
✔ Hong Kong casualty: down 11% to 20%, or HK$110,000 to HK$200,000 on a HK$1 million premium
✔ Japan, and motor in Japan and Korea: up 1% to 10%

If you are renewing in a soft market, are you asking for better terms or only a lower price?
